## Phase & Sponsor Analysis (10 Affordable Prosthetic Studies)

### Phase Breakdown

| Phase | Count | % | Assessment |
|-------|-------|---|------------|
| **N/A** | 9 | 90% | Device evaluations, feasibility studies, observational research |
| **Phase 3** | 1 | 10% | VA/Michigan CESR foot trial — only traditional Phase 3 in dataset |

**Key Insight:** 90% of affordable prosthetics research is at the "N/A" phase — typical for device, behavioral, and intervention studies that don't follow traditional pharmaceutical trial phases. These are primarily feasibility/pilot studies, device evaluations, and observational research. Only 1 study (NCT00494143, the VA/Michigan CESR foot trial) reached Phase 3, indicating this is the most clinically advanced prosthetics study in the dataset and the only one with Level 1 evidence. The field remains largely in early-stage evidence generation — a major opportunity for community resource coordination to connect patients with emerging technologies while they await Phase 3 validation.

**Key Insight:** Academic institutions dominate (60%), which is encouraging for affordability — academic studies tend to focus on cost-effective solutions rather than maximizing profit. Federal sponsors (20%, both VA) are the most significant funders for US amputee populations — VA insurance covers 100% of prosthetic costs for eligible veterans. Industry sponsors (20%) bring commercial innovation, particularly in myoelectric control systems. International public health sponsors (INAIL, Italy) provide a model for equitable access that could be replicated in community programs.

### Most Promising Sponsors for Affordable Prosthetics — Ranked

1. **Academic/Research Hospitals** (60%) — Shirley Ryan AbilityLab, Medical University of Vienna, CHU de Nîmes, INAIL Bologna. These institutions drive the most innovative low-cost solutions: 3D-printed sockets, rapid fabrication, and novel socket materials. They also provide care regardless of insurance status.

2. **Federal (VA/DoD)** (20%) — The VA system provides free prosthetic care for all eligible veterans, making it the most accessible system in the US. VA-funded studies (NCT03828331, NCT00494143) directly serve the largest amputee population in the country. Every study design considers real-world clinical implementation.

3. **Industry (Startups)** (20%) — Coapt (adaptive control) and Infinite Biomedical Technologies (Glide strategy) are focused on solving the #1 barrier to prosthetic use: difficult calibration and control adjustment. Their innovations directly reduce lifetime cost by reducing clinic visits.

4. **International Public Health** (10%) — INAIL (Italy) addresses prosthetic access for workplace injuries, providing a model for universal prosthetic coverage that advocacy organizations can reference.

## Most Promising Trends for Affordable Prosthetics (Confirmed by 10 Studies)

1. **3D printing for affordability** — NCT06938087 (PROFIL foot) and NCT04725461 (foam socket) show 3D-printed components can reduce costs by 50–80% and fabrication time from days to ~90 minutes
2. **Rapid socket fabrication** — The Shirley Ryan AbilityLab foam method (NCT04725461) enables same-day socket fitting — eliminates multiple clinic visits and material waste
3. **Adaptive myoelectric control** — NCT04272489 (Coapt) and NCT07222085 (IBT Glide) both address the #1 barrier to prosthetic use: difficult calibration. Simpler control = fewer clinic visits = lower lifetime cost
4. **Hybrid/powered legs** — NCT04038151 (Shirley Ryan AbilityLab) demonstrates that lightweight powered knees (~1.7 kg) can improve community mobility, reducing long-term disability costs
5. **Osseointegration** — NCT06737770 shows direct skeletal attachment eliminates socket-related complications (34–63% of users), potentially reducing long-term prosthetic management costs
6. **Veterans-focused rehabilitation** — NCT03828331 and NCT00494143 (both VA) demonstrate that optimized training and equipment can improve outcomes for the large and growing veteran amputee population
7. **Standardized outcome measurement** — NCT03329326 provides validated tools (SHAP, DASH, SF-36, ACMC) needed to compare affordable vs. premium prosthetics — essential for evidence-based, cost-effective care
8. **Home-based rehabilitation** — Multiple studies demonstrate that app-based training, virtual peer coaching, and home trials can improve outcomes without costly in-person clinic visits
9. **Energy-storing prosthetic feet** — NCT00494143 (CESR foot) and NCT06938087 (PROFIL foot) both show that energy return technology reduces the metabolic cost of walking by up to 50%, directly improving quality of life
10. **Motor learning gait training** — Error-manipulation approaches (NCT03828331) may replace expensive lifelong gait training with short, targeted programs

## Why Shirley Ryan AbilityLab?

Shirley Ryan AbilityLab is the #1 ranked rehabilitation hospital in the US (U.S. News & World Report) and the only site leading 2 of the 10 studies reviewed (NCT04725461 and NCT04038151). Key advantages:

- **Established Amputee Center** — One of the largest in the country; comprehensive prosthetic services including same-day socket adjustments
- **Regenstein Center for Bionic Medicine** — World leader in powered prosthetic research; home of the Hybrid Leg trial (NCT04038151)
- **Academic affiliation** — Physical Medicine & Rehabilitation home of Northwestern University; access to cutting-edge research
- **Location** — Streeterville, Chicago: 98/100 Walk Score; 6 hospitals within 1.5 km; 7 pharmacies within 1.5 km; CTA Red/Brown/Purple lines within 500m
- **Financial support** — On-site financial counselors for prosthetic coverage appeals; billing assistance for Medicare/Medicaid/VA

## NOTE: Real-World Resources for Amputees

Provision of affordable prosthetics depends on more than clinical trials. Amputees need access to:
- A hospital with a prosthetics program (Shirley Ryan AbilityLab, Jesse Brown VA)
- A pharmacy carrying prosthetic supplies (skin care, liner socks, creams — 7 within 1.5 km)
- Public transit to attend appointments (CTA Red/Brown/Purple + buses)
- Peer support and community (Shirley Ryan Amputee Support Group; Amputee Coalition of America)
- Financial assistance for uncovered costs (Shirley Ryan Financial Counselor; Amputee Coalition; Limbs International)
